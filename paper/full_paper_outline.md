# Full-paper outline

1. **Research question.** Distinguish current hitter ability from adjustment with repeated exposure. Explain how swing geometry can change during repeated whiffs and define what public tracking can identify.
2. **Related work and contribution.** Powers/Yurko's context-conditioned swing metrics; Prasad's pitch sequences; Steinhardt/Borowiak's direct adaptation study. Contribution: mechanical deviation plus explicit player uncertainty, negative controls and temporal validation.
3. **Data availability and coverage.** Sample accounting, replayable requests, coverage by outcome/player/type/year, new tracking measurements on whiffs, retrospective backfill, 2026 reference changes.
4. **Measurement.** Held-out current-pitch swing expectations; circular direction; covariance-calibrated deviation and physical components. Whiff and miss-distance validity, complete-case limitations.
5. **Pitch history.** Paired current/history predictive comparison with uncertainty. Trajectory features and confounding. Explain the limits of causal interpretation for predictive improvements.
6. **Adaptation estimator.** Exposure definitions, nuisance residualization, cluster-level information, hierarchical measurement error, prior sensitivity, model scope and estimability.
7. **Probabilistic comparison.** Joint draws, pairwise probabilities and ranks. Compare uncertainty for hitters with different sample sizes and avoid unsupported talent rankings.
8. **Validation.** Simulated-data parameter recovery, zero-learning simulations, calibration, posterior predictive checks, temporal measurement-error correlation and conditional predictive transport.
9. **Falsification.** Future/irrelevant/shuffled exposure, changing difficulty, selection, similar-shape restrictions, alternate components, functional forms and within-game memory assumptions.
10. **Exploratory percentile stability.** Explain the rank-invariance result, complete 84-specification search, unpooled and pooled scores, percentile groups, high-information restriction, multiplicity correction and internal early/late replication. Preserve the distinction from the original temporal validation.
11. **Potential applications.** Identify uncertainty and hypotheses for further player-development measurement. State the validated scope of future-performance results and the additional evidence required for player decisions.
12. **Limitations and further identification research.** Stronger sequential ignorability, dynamic pitch selection, instrument error, missingness, first-stage uncertainty and externally validated structural learning models.
13. **Reproducibility appendix.** Frozen source and analytic datasets, checksums, code, pinned environment, notebook and script entry points, data rights, and publication instructions.
