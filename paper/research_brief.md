# 1. Central research objective

Develop and empirically test a framework for measuring **batter adaptation as a latent baseball skill**.

The central question is:

> **How quickly does a hitter correct mechanical swing errors after exposure to a pitch, pitch shape, pitcher, or pitch sequence, and can that rate of adaptation be estimated as a persistent player-level probability distribution?**

The project should estimate both:

1. **Pitch-sequence-induced swing distortion**
   
   How preceding pitches alter the geometry, timing, and quality of the batter's next swing after conditioning on the characteristics of the current pitch.

2. **Batter adaptation / error correction**
   
   How rapidly the batter reduces those induced errors after repeated exposure.

The second contribution is the primary focus of the paper.

The ultimate goal is not simply to say:

> Batter A adapts faster than Batter B.

Instead, develop a statistically rigorous latent-skill model that produces distributions such as:

\[
p(\lambda_i \mid \text{data})
\]

where \(\lambda_i\) represents the underlying adaptation ability of hitter \(i\).

The framework should support probabilistic comparative statements such as:

\[
P(\lambda_i > \lambda_j \mid \text{data})
\]

and

\[
P(\lambda_i > \lambda_{\text{league median}}\mid\text{data}).
\]

It should also quantify uncertainty about the rank of each hitter:

\[
p(Rank_i=r\mid\text{data}).
\]

The paper should treat adaptation as a **latent player skill measured with uncertainty**, not as a noisy leaderboard statistic.

A useful conceptual decomposition is:

previous pitch history  
→ unexpected swing distortion  
→ repeated exposure  
→ error correction  
→ latent batter adaptation ability.

Possible working titles:

- **Learning to Adjust: Measuring Batter Adaptation from MLB Bat Tracking**
- **Set Up and Adjust: Pitch-Induced Swing Distortion and Batter Adaptation**
- **Learning to Hit the Unhittable: Estimating Batter Error-Correction Skill**
- **Who Adjusts Fastest? A Probabilistic Model of Batter Adaptation**

Do not lock the title until we see the results.

# 3. Scientific thesis

Most baseball evaluation systems measure a player's current ability.

This project asks whether there is another persistent skill:

\[
\boxed{\text{How quickly does the player learn from what he has just seen?}}
\]

Traditional outcomes make this difficult to observe.

A hitter can swing and miss at the same slider three times while progressively getting closer to making contact.

Traditional data records:

\[
Whiff,\ Whiff,\ Whiff.
\]

Bat tracking may instead reveal:

\[
Error_1 > Error_2 > Error_3.
\]

The player may therefore be adapting before conventional outcomes improve.

The project should attempt to infer this hidden process.

The ideal contribution is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Observed swings}
\rightarrow
\text{estimated swing error}
\rightarrow
\text{error correction}
\rightarrow
\text{posterior distribution over adaptation skill}
}
\]

The scientific standard should be high enough that we can distinguish:

- real adaptation;
- regression to the mean;
- easier subsequent pitches;
- changing counts;
- pitcher behavior;
- pitch-selection endogeneity;
- survivorship from repeated exposure;
- random mechanical variation.

# 8. Define the hitter's expected swing

Raw swing metrics are not errors.

The correct swing depends on the pitch.

For swing \(t\), define observed swing state:

\[
S_t.
\]

Candidate dimensions:

\[
S_t=
[
attack\ angle,
attack\ direction,
swing\ path\ tilt,
intercept_x,
intercept_y,
bat\ speed,
swing\ length
].
\]

Do not mechanically include every field.

Select dimensions based on:
- coverage;
- measurement properties;
- interpretability;
- stability.

Estimate:

\[
E[S_t\mid X_t,i]
\]

where \(X_t\) includes information about the current pitch and game state, and \(i\) identifies the hitter.

Inputs may include:

- pitch trajectory;
- pitch location;
- pitch type;
- velocity;
- count;
- handedness;
- hitter identity.

Do NOT include previous-pitch information in this baseline.

Use out-of-fold predictions.

Candidate modeling approaches:

- Bayesian hierarchical regression;
- hierarchical GAM;
- multivariate hierarchical model;
- flexible ML combined with cross-fitting.

Prefer a model whose residuals remain interpretable.

Define:

\[
R_t=S_t-\widehat E[S_t\mid X_t,i].
\]

This is the batter's **unexpected swing deviation**.

# 11. Main research question: estimate adaptation as a latent player skill

This is the centerpiece.

We want to infer a hitter-specific latent quantity:

\[
\lambda_i
\]

representing the player's ability to correct swing errors after exposure.

The observed data are noisy measurements of that latent ability.

Use a **hierarchical Bayesian model** or comparably rigorous probabilistic framework.

The model should produce the full posterior:

\[
p(\lambda_i\mid D).
\]

Do not reduce a player to a point estimate.

The model should partially pool players toward the population distribution.

A basic starting point could resemble:

\[
Error_{i,e}
=
\alpha_i+
f(exposure_{i,e};\lambda_i)
+
\beta^\top X_{i,e}
+
\epsilon_{i,e}.
\]

Potential functional forms include:

### Linear correction

\[
Error_{i,e}
=
\alpha_i-\lambda_i exposure_{i,e}+\epsilon.
\]

### Exponential correction

\[
Error_{i,e}
=
A_i e^{-\lambda_i exposure_{i,e}}+C_i+\epsilon.
\]

### State-space learning

\[
z_{i,e+1}
=
z_{i,e}
-\lambda_i z_{i,e}
+
\eta_{i,e}
\]

with observed swing error:

\[
Error_{i,e}
=
z_{i,e}
+
\nu_{i,e}.
\]

### Bayesian learning model

Treat the hitter as updating an internal belief about pitch behavior after each observation.

Explore whether this provides a more meaningful structural interpretation than simple exposure-count regression.

Do not choose the most sophisticated model merely because it is sophisticated.

Compare plausible specifications through:
- predictive performance;
- calibration;
- posterior predictive checks;
- parameter recovery simulations;
- interpretability.

# 13. Define exposure carefully

A major methodological problem is deciding when two pitches count as repeated exposure.

Test multiple definitions.

## Exact pitch type

Example:
slider → slider.

## Pitcher-specific pitch

Same hitter, same pitcher, same pitch type.

## Shape-based similarity

Represent pitches in a continuous feature space using:

- velocity;
- IVB;
- horizontal movement;
- release point;
- VAA;
- HAA;
- location where appropriate.

Define a similarity kernel:

\[
w_{ab}=\exp(-d(P_a,P_b)^2/h).
\]

Then exposure does not have to be binary.

A previous pitch can contribute partial informational exposure according to its similarity to the current pitch.

Potentially define effective cumulative exposure:

\[
E_t=
\sum_{s<t}K(P_s,P_t)\cdot decay(t-s).
\]

This may be statistically more rigorous than simply counting “number of sliders seen.”

Tune kernel bandwidth and memory decay out of sample.

Estimate whether meaningful learning persists:

- within PA;
- within game;
- within series;

# 23. Regression-to-the-mean problem

This is especially important.

A large first-exposure error will mechanically tend to be followed by a smaller error even with zero true learning.

Do NOT call that adaptation.

Required safeguards should include some combination of:

- hierarchical state-space model;
- explicit measurement-error model;
- conditioning on initial latent state rather than raw observed error;
- simulations under zero adaptation;
- placebo exposure assignments;
- shuffled sequence histories.

Demonstrate that the estimator does not infer artificial adaptation when data are generated with:

\[
\lambda_i=0.
\]

# 24. Placebo tests

Run strong negative controls.

### Shuffled exposure history

Randomly permute relevant historical pitches while respecting game/context structure.

Estimated adaptation should disappear.

### Future pitch placebo

Future pitches cannot cause prior adaptation.

Using future exposure should not generate equivalent effects.

### Simulated no-learning world

Generate data with true:

\[
\lambda_i=0.
\]

The model should recover a population centered near zero.

### Irrelevant-pitch exposure

Exposure to physically dissimilar pitches should produce much weaker adaptation effects than exposure to similar pitches.

# 41. Competitive SSAC standard

Do not optimize merely for completing an analysis.

The paper should have a clear answer to:

> **What can an MLB team now know about a hitter that it could not know from conventional player statistics?**

The intended answer is something like:

> We can estimate not only how a hitter performs against a pitch, but how quickly he learns to correct the mechanical error that pitch induces.

Potential applications:

### Player evaluation
Distinguish hitters with similar current ability but different learning/adaptation characteristics.

### Player development
Identify hitters who struggle to correct specific classes of swing error.

### Advance scouting
Estimate how quickly an opposing hitter is likely to solve a pitch shape or sequencing strategy.

### Pitching strategy
Understand how long a sequencing advantage is likely to persist before a particular hitter adjusts.

### Player projections
Investigate whether adaptability improves projections of future performance, especially for younger or developing hitters.

# 42. Minimum successful result

The project is successful if it establishes:

### A.
Repeated exposure is associated with measurable reduction in appropriately defined swing error after conditioning on current-pitch characteristics.

### B.
The adaptation signal survives serious controls for regression to the mean and changing pitch quality.

### C.
A hierarchical model can estimate hitter-specific adaptation with meaningful uncertainty.

### D.
The posterior distributions support calibrated probabilistic comparisons between hitters.

### E.
At least some component of adaptation demonstrates out-of-sample persistence, OR the analysis establishes that individual differences are too uncertain to support a stable player-skill interpretation.

A null result on E must be reported honestly.

The project should distinguish between:

\[
\text{evidence that hitters adapt in aggregate}
\]

and

\[
\text{evidence that adaptation is a persistent individual skill}.
\]

Those are not the same claim.

# 44. Research philosophy

Prioritize inference over leaderboard creation.

The most important output is not:

> Player X ranks first.

It is:

\[
P(\lambda_X>\lambda_Y\mid D)=p
\]

with a defensible interpretation of what \(\lambda\) represents.

Treat:
- adaptation;
- skill uncertainty;
- measurement error;
- repeated exposure;
- pitcher response;
- regression to the mean

as part of the same statistical problem.

A model that creates attractive rankings without convincing identification and calibration is not acceptable.

The goal is a result that is:

- new;
- statistically rigorous;
- physically interpretable;
- probabilistic;
- useful to baseball teams;
- fully reproducible;
- competitive at MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference.
