# Deja Swing: methods and identification

## Estimand

The estimand is a hitter's **conditional association between prior exposure and unexpected swing deviation**. Its causal interpretation as a motor-learning rate is not identified. Positive lambda means a decrease in deviation, in calibration-period standard deviations, per unit increase in log(1 + prior exposures). Because exposure is log-transformed, the modeled difference between zero and one prior pitch is lambda times log(2). Negative values are allowed. The parameter does not measure the percentage of error eliminated.

The requested target is regular-season MLB pitches from July 14, 2023 through execution in September 2026. The raw snapshot can include September 21; the analytic cutoff is September 20, the last completed calendar day. Exact dates, retrieval timestamps, request URLs, row counts, independent schedule comparisons, and hashes accompany the frozen files. The analysis uses retroactively backfilled geometry for retrospective temporal validation. These exports may not have been available in their current form during 2023.

The observations are public MLB Baseball Savant records, attributed to MLB. This project's MIT license applies to its original code. MLB's data and other parties' work retain their upstream rights and terms. Public access does not authorize the project to relicense these materials. Provenance and applicable upstream terms should accompany the frozen data.

## Inclusion, history, and measurement

The pipeline downloads all pitch-level records, retains automatic-ball/automatic-strike events in the raw audit, and excludes those administrative events before constructing physical exposure histories. It sorts within games by plate appearance and pitch number and constructs histories before selecting swings. Takes and missing bat observations count as exposure. Non-regular-season records and duplicate pitch identifiers are rejected or resolved explicitly at ingestion. The broad feasibility audit includes bunt attempts; the model uses non-bunt swing/contact descriptions, 20–110 mph bat speed and 1–12 foot swing length with complete five-component tracking. These declared bounds screen for implausible instrument values independently of pitch outcomes. A >=50 mph sensitivity restriction checks sensitivity to partial swings. Whiffs include swinging strikes and blocked swinging strikes; foul tips count as contact. Rare pitchouts are outside the modeling sample. Aggregate sample and exclusion counts are logged. The adaptation analysis additionally requires a valid current pitch shape.

Within-PA histories reset at PA boundaries. Exposure definitions include same pitcher and type within the game (primary), exact type within PA, exact type within game, and repeated transitions. The within-game continuous kernel uses velocity, horizontal/vertical movement and release x/z; fixed scales are 5 mph and 0.5 feet for each remaining dimension. Similarity is exp(-squared standardized distance / bandwidth), multiplied by exp(-pitch-opportunity lag * log(2)/half-life). Sensitivity analyses use bandwidths 0.5, 1, and 2 and half-lives 5 and 20. These values are fixed rather than optimized. The index advances when this hitter sees a pitch. Kernels reset each game; they do not estimate forgetting in seconds, across series, or across seasons. Invalid current shapes produce missing familiarity values. Invalid historical shapes are skipped. No player-specific forgetting parameter is claimed.

Trajectory position is reconstructed from constant-acceleration coefficients referenced at y=50 feet, anchored to exported plate location. Separations at y=23.8 and 5 feet and two-dimensional x/z release separation describe pitch trajectories. They do not measure perceptual pitch tunnels exactly. Invalid flight solutions are missing. For 2026 the export anchor shifts from the front (17 inches) to the middle (8.5 inches), consistent with the CSV documentation. Current and lagged plate locations are harmonized to the front plane using the fitted trajectory. ABS also changes exported zone bounds; plane harmonization does not remove that remaining discontinuity in normalized zone inputs.

## Expected swings and deviation

Current-pitch CatBoost models use front-plane-harmonized location, velocity, movement, release, spin, trajectory approach angles, count, handedness, pitcher/batter identifiers, home team as a park proxy, score context, inning, and day of season. They explicitly exclude prior pitches, exposure, pitch results, future schedule fields, and batted-ball outcomes. Historical and current models use identical fitting budgets. Categorical identities are learned only in the fit sample. Training is capped by deterministic random sampling for accessible CPU reproduction.

Both current-only and history-augmented models train on July 2023–June 2024, with residual covariance calibrated independently on July–September 2024. All analysis predictions in 2025 and 2026 are therefore out of training. Attack direction is predicted through sine/cosine coordinates and reconstructed with atan2; its residual is the shortest signed circular difference. The physical residual vector contains attack angle, attack direction, swing-path tilt, bat speed and swing length. Intercept fields and miss distance are audited but are not mechanically added to this vector. Miss distance is treated as an outcome observed primarily on whiffs, never imputed as zero for contact.

Calibration residuals are divided by their calibration SDs, then a Ledoit-Wolf covariance and mean are fitted. Mahalanobis distance from this residual distribution is centered/scaled with calibration-only moments. Component residuals remain available in physical units. The score measures atypicality relative to expected mechanics. It does not identify an optimal swing. A second-stage held-out whiff classifier tests whether the residual components contain information about bat-ball interaction beyond current pitch context. This contemporaneous association does not turn deviations into causal mistakes or provide pre-pitch forecasts.

Sequence distortion is the paired loss difference between current-only and history-augmented models. Game-cluster resampling gives 90% intervals for percentage MSE reduction. The original primary model hyperparameters and covariance definition were fixed before inspecting 2026 outcomes. Additional specifications chosen after inspecting 2026 are exploratory and do not share that validation status.

## Adaptation slope and partial pooling

Exposure is endogenous. Separate regressions adjust deviation and exposure for current-pitch and sequence features, including pitcher and batter identities. In 2025 these nuisance predictions use three out-of-fold groups of complete games. These folds implement contemporaneous cross-fitting and can use later games within 2025 for training. Nuisance predictions in 2026 are trained only on 2025. No history feature is constructed from future rows; the future-count variable is a segregated negative-control response, never an input.

For each period and hitter, the analysis centers the nuisance residuals, regresses residual deviation on residual exposure, and reverses the coefficient's sign. Standard errors use the sandwich variance clustered by game. Eligibility requires at least 40 observations, 8 games and residual exposure information >=1 to support a Gaussian approximation and estimability. Eligible players can still differ in estimation precision. Counts and information are reported. Player intercepts are allowed in each period. The first-stage estimator resembles a partially linear, cross-fitted slope; it remains sensitive to nuisance misspecification and unmeasured pitcher strategy.

The second-stage measurement-error model is

`estimated_lambda_i | lambda_i ~ Normal(lambda_i, se_i^2)`

`lambda_i | mu, tau ~ Normal(mu, tau^2)`

`mu ~ Normal(0, 0.2^2); tau ~ HalfNormal(0.15)`.

The continuous prior on tau has no point mass at exactly zero. A central credible interval for tau excludes zero by construction and must not be treated as a test establishing player heterogeneity. The substantive checks are predictive improvement, recovery, interval width, and temporal reliability, with their stated limitations.

The model integrates mu analytically and tau by one-dimensional quadrature with an adaptive upper bound. Independent conditional draws propagate hyperparameter uncertainty. Grid-tail mass, grid spacing and double-grid numerical differences are saved. R-hat, chain ESS and divergences are inapplicable because this is not MCMC; Monte Carlo error still applies. The normal first-stage approximation treats SEs as known and omits uncertainty in nuisance/expected-swing fits and cross-hitter shared-game dependence. The resulting posterior approximates first-stage uncertainty and is not a full joint swing-level posterior.

Draw-wise comparisons use the same joint posterior: probabilities above population mean, selected-player median, quartiles and deciles; arbitrary pairwise probabilities; and rank distributions. The population consists of estimable hitters in that period, so results do not generalize to every MLB hitter or minor-league prospect. Rank distributions retain uncertainty and do not establish ordinal talent rankings.

## Validation and falsification

1. Simulated scalar swing-level outcomes have unequal counts, cluster-level noise, exposure-dependent missingness under a stated MAR mechanism, heterogeneous slopes or exactly zero learning. A correctly specified linear first stage adjusts observed pitch difficulty, followed by the same measurement-error hierarchy. Known synthetic truth evaluates interval coverage, bias, RMSE, ranks and pairwise calibration across independent experiments. Finite-cluster undercoverage is reported. These simulations do not reconstruct raw swing geometry or refit the full CatBoost/Mahalanobis pipeline; they validate a simpler first-stage setting. They do not prove empirical MLB calibration or protect against MNAR data.
2. Measurement-error-aware two-period bivariate-normal likelihood estimates persistence from unpooled slopes and their SEs; a player bootstrap gives uncertainty. Correlations at a variance boundary are explicitly weakly identified. Separately, 2025 individual slopes predict centered 2026 residuals against a league-slope baseline, with game-bootstrap loss intervals. Held-out centering tests conditional slope transport. It does not evaluate prospective predictions of raw swings.
3. Future-pitch counts, irrelevant-type counts and within-hitter-game shuffled exposure residuals diagnose selection/timing confounding. A future association cannot establish causality; an equally strong placebo undermines an adaptation interpretation.
4. Exposure definitions, fixed kernel scales, component-specific absolute residuals, linear and fixed-saturation exposure transforms, prior scales, early innings and recent similar-shape restrictions are reported. These analyses assess sensitivity to the specification. Linear/saturation coefficients have different units and cannot be compared numerically to log-exposure rates as though identical.
5. Selection tables show changing pitch quality, counts, inning, sample size and hitter population over exposure. Restrictions probe robustness but do not solve endogenous selection or pitchers changing strategy. Missingness by player/type/season is measured before complete-case selection. No inverse-probability model is claimed to remove unobserved selection.
6. Exploratory future whiff and batted-ball xwOBA regressions compare prior performance with prior performance plus adaptation, cross-validating held-out players. The xwOBA endpoint explicitly requires `description == hit_into_play`: the exported expected-wOBA field also contains strikeout zeros, which must not enter a contact-quality average. Predictors come from 2025, but regression coefficients are trained using 2026 outcomes for the other player folds. This evaluates player-held-out prediction. The forecasting model is not fully trained before 2026. Both seasons' outcomes are restricted to the eligible tracked-swing sample, so they are not unrestricted player-season batting statistics. These analyses do not establish causal utility and do not fully propagate adaptation posterior uncertainty.

## Interpretation and identification limits

No structural motor-learning, state-space retention, individual forgetting, or pitcher-deception parameter is identified. The hierarchy measures conditional exposure slopes. There is no guarantee that reducing swing atypicality is beneficial. Aggregate association, individual heterogeneity, persistent skill, simulation calibration, and causal adaptation are distinct evidentiary questions. A large aggregate association accompanied by failed negative controls remains confounded even when posterior intervals are narrow. Full identification would require stronger designs, richer measurement and external validation.

## Exploratory percentile comparisons

After inspecting the original 2026 results, a separate search assessed whether rank relationships were more stable under different score definitions. The fixed family includes 84 specifications, two score implementations, and five rank or group endpoints. All combinations, intervals, negative results, and BH/BY multiplicity adjustments are retained. Posterior means are integrated deterministically to avoid Monte Carlo variation in tightly pooled rankings. This changes score computation for the exploratory comparisons; it does not replace the primary posterior draws.

Early-season partitions select one candidate before its late-season internal replication is computed. The underlying observations were already used in full-year analyses, and the partitions share nuisance functions, so this check is not independent external validation. Player-bootstrap intervals condition on fitted scores and percentile definitions. Stable playing time or measurement precision can contribute to stable pooled ranks. The [percentile report](percentile_stability.md) records the complete specification, selection rule, results, and limitations. Converting the same score to percentiles cannot change its Spearman or Kendall correlation.

## Whiff-distance sensitivity

A supplementary model applies the same nuisance residualization and slope hierarchy to log(1 + recorded miss distance), standardized with 2025 moments, among tracked whiffs. Contacts are neither included as zero nor treated as observed distances. This directly examines a bat-ball proximity endpoint but remains selected on missing contact and can change as marginal whiffs become contact. It is not an unconditional error-correction estimate. Units are standardized log export values; unverified physical units are not inferred from column names.

## Shared model error and measurement drift

Both periods share the earlier expected-swing fit, and 2026 nuisance predictions are learned from 2025. Correlated model error can therefore induce apparent cross-period persistence, while the simple measurement-error correlation likelihood assumes independent known-SE errors. Positive correlation supports stability of the conditional metric rather than identifying motor-learning ability. The calibration-standardized deviation mean and SD are reported by season in baseline_drift.csv; centering removes a global intercept shift but not all heterogeneous drift. Powers/Yurko discusses contact/closest-approach sampling for bat speed and length. Public angle/tilt definitions are phrased at contact and do not fully establish the corresponding reference instant on a miss. Whiff geometry is therefore interpreted as exported tracking descriptors. It has not been calibrated as geometric error, and contemporaneous whiff associations may partly reflect measurement timing.

## Nuisance computation specification

Before inspecting player-slope or temporal-persistence estimates, nuisance categorical target-statistic combinations were limited to two-way combinations (`max_ctr_complexity=2`) following CatBoost CPU guidance. This changes regularization from the implicit four-way default and therefore changes the model specification. All 20 response targets, 150,000-row cap, depth-six trees, 180 boosting iterations and current/history controls were retained. Eight CPU threads accelerate fitting; baseline swing models retain their original specification. Cross-fitted 2025 prediction MSE/R-squared is logged for every nuisance target. No 2026 player result selected this setting.
